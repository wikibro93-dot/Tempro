You paste a temporary address into a signup form and get a short refusal: this domain is not allowed. The site has simply decided not to accept addresses from domains it recognises as disposable. The reason for the block also tells you which responses are reasonable.

## Why sites block disposable domains

A site offering a free trial, a download, or a community account faces a spam problem. Disposable addresses make it easy to create many accounts quickly, claim the same offer repeatedly, or post without accountability. Blocking known disposable domains is a blunt but simple defence against that abuse.

Sites detect these addresses by keeping lists of domains known to offer temporary mail, checking a domain's history and mail setup, or using screening services that score an address. A widely shared temporary domain is more likely to appear on those lists.

## What a block is telling you

Treat a block as information about that signup, not as a puzzle to solve at any cost. Ask one question first: does this account matter in the long term? If the answer is yes, the block may be doing you a favour. Accounts you will need later, for password resets, order history, refunds, or support, are a poor fit for a temporary inbox anyway. A temporary inbox expires, and expired inboxes and messages are assumed unrecoverable in TempMail Pro. If you cannot get back in without that inbox, you should not have used it for that account.

If the answer is no, and the signup is a one-time download, a short trial, or a newsletter you only want to sample, a legitimate alternative may be worth trying, within the site's rules.

## Legitimate options, in order

Start by reading the message on screen, and check whether the site offers a guest, preview, or alternative sign-in option. Use that option before creating any account if one exists.

If the site allows disposable mail in principle but has blocked one particular domain, you may try a different live domain offered by TempMail Pro, if one is available. This is only reasonable when you are not trying to get around a rule that applies to you personally, such as a ban, a trial limit, or a one-per-person offer. Trying a different domain to claim a second trial you are not entitled to, or to return after a ban, is evasion, and it can get the new account closed as well.

If no temporary domain is accepted, or if you are unsure whether trying another one is allowed, stop there and use a real address you control. A permanent address, or an alias on a mailbox you own, gives you recovery options and keeps you inside the site's terms. You can still protect your main inbox with a dedicated signup address or a filter that files promotional mail away. For a fuller comparison, see [temporary email compared with an email alias](/blog/temp-email-vs-email-alias).

## What not to do

Do not keep cycling through domains, addresses, and browsers to wear a site down. Repeated attempts can look like automated abuse, even if your intent was ordinary, and they can trigger a wider block on your connection.

Do not use false personal details to get past a check. A signup that requires a real identity, a payment method, or a verified phone number is signalling that it is not a casual, anonymous signup. Invented details create an account you cannot honestly maintain.

Do not buy, borrow, or share someone else's address to pass a check, and do not follow online advice that tells you to disguise a disposable address or rotate domains to dodge a limit. That advice asks you to break the site's rules.

## A checklist for a blocked signup

- Note the exact error message, so you are not guessing about what was blocked.
- Decide whether this account needs recovery later. If it does, use a permanent address now.
- Check for a guest, preview, or alternative sign-in option.
- If the signup is genuinely one-time and disposable mail is not banned for you personally, try one different live domain only, if one is offered.
- If that also fails, switch to a real address you control, or walk away from the signup.
- Save any code or confirmation you need before a temporary inbox expires.

Most blocked signups resolve at the second or fifth step. The ones that do not are usually signups that were never a good fit for a temporary inbox.
