## The shopping angle this guide covers: the promo funnel

A companion article covers shopping with temp mail in general — one-off purchases, guest checkout, and keeping receipts separate from marketing. This guide covers a narrower, more spam-prone job: the promotional funnel itself. Flash sales, first-order coupons, seasonal campaigns, and marketplace signups are designed to keep emailing you long after the discount is spent. Used deliberately, a temporary inbox lets you take the offer without joining the list.

## How a promo funnel actually works

A typical funnel runs in stages, and each stage sends mail:

1. **The hook:** a coupon code or flash-sale access, in exchange for your address.
2. **The nudge:** abandoned-cart reminders and "offer ends soon" messages over the next few days.
3. **The retention push:** weekly sale alerts, loyalty invitations, and seasonal campaigns, indefinitely.

Your personal inbox only needs stage 1. Stages 2 and 3 are the spam. A temporary inbox collects all three stages in isolation, and when it expires, the funnel ends — no unsubscribe forms, no "are you sure?" screens, no partner lists to chase.

## A promo-funnel workflow

**Separate the funnel from the purchase.** Use one temporary inbox for coupon unlocks and sale alerts, and keep order confirmations somewhere you can find them. If the purchase itself matters — a warranty, a return window, a delivery problem — save the order number and receipt to your notes immediately, or route that purchase through a stable address instead. The general shopping guide's warning still applies: warranty-critical purchases do not belong on a disposable inbox.

**One inbox per campaign, not per store.** A store running a spring sale, a flash sale, and a loyalty signup is really running three funnels. One inbox per campaign means you can retire the flash-sale funnel the day it ends while keeping the loyalty one alive a little longer, instead of killing all three at once.

**Time the expiry to the campaign, not to the code.** A coupon code arrives in minutes, but its abandoned-cart reminders arrive over days and its "last chance" email arrives near the deadline. A 24-hour or 7-day expiry matches a campaign; a 10-minute expiry matches only the code. For a US seasonal sale — holiday promotions, back-to-school offers, clearance events — check the sale's end date first, then pick an expiry that covers it plus a day or two for delayed shipping mail.

**Track what you unlocked.** Keep a one-line note per campaign: store, campaign, inbox name, code received, expiry. When a "your code expires tomorrow" message arrives in the right inbox, you will know whether you already used the code or still plan to.

## Flash sales, marketplaces, and seasonal signups

**Flash sales** are the best fit for this approach, because urgency is the point: you want access now, and you do not want weekly reminders after the sale. A short-to-medium expiry — 24 hours to a few days — covers the sale window and its reminders, then ends.

**Marketplace one-time purchases** work similarly, with one extra caution: marketplace messages include seller communication and delivery updates, which can stretch over days. Give those inboxes a longer window than a pure coupon signup, and save tracking numbers to your notes as soon as they arrive.

**Seasonal campaign signups** — holiday catalogs, seasonal lookbooks, event-driven offers — are the worst spam source relative to value, because they recur every season. A temporary inbox per season keeps each year's campaign isolated, so last year's holiday list cannot follow you into this year.

## When to promote a store out of the funnel

Not every store should stay quarantined. If you order from a store regularly, if you need its support, or if you want its loyalty benefits long-term, move it to an email alias or your permanent address before the temporary inbox expires. The test is simple: would losing this address cost you an order, a refund, or an account you care about? If yes, promote it. If no, let it expire. ## Mistakes in promo-funnel shopping

- **Using the funnel inbox for the order itself.** Then the order, the receipt, and the return window all die with the funnel. Separate the discount from the purchase record.
- **One inbox for every sale.** Mixed campaigns cannot be retired individually, and a single inbox full of competing "last chance" emails is unreadable.
- **Forgetting the code you unlocked.** A coupon saved only in an expired inbox is a coupon lost. Copy codes to your notes at once.
- **Treating a marketplace account like a coupon.** An account you will reuse for selling, messaging, or repeat buying needs a stable address, not a funnel inbox.

## Bottom line

Temporary email is at its best in shopping not as a general purchase tool, but as a funnel quarantine: take the coupon, let the campaign run its course in isolation, and let the inbox expire when the campaign ends. Promote the stores that earn it; let the rest go quiet on their own.
