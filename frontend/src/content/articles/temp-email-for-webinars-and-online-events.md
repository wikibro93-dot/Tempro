A webinar registration asks for an email address, then sends a chain of messages: a confirmation, a reminder the day before, a joining link an hour before, and often a recording link after the event. That chain is useful, but it also adds you to a mailing list you may not want. Temporary email offers a middle path: register, receive the chain, attend, and let the inbox expire when the event is over.

That only works if the inbox is still alive on event day. A 10-minute inbox that captured the confirmation will be gone long before a webinar scheduled for next Thursday. The core skill is matching expiry to the event date, and keeping the joining link somewhere reachable.

## Why events are different

Most temporary email use is short: a code, a download, a one-time form. Events stretch over days or weeks, with registration now, attendance later, and possibly a recording after that.

An event inbox needs to cover four moments: confirmation, reminders, the joining link, and any follow-up such as slides or a recording. TempMail Pro offers 10 minutes, 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days, or a custom duration up to 365 days, with no sign-in required. Think in event time, not signup time.

## Named platforms: how each one sends the link

The chain of messages is not identical on every platform. Knowing what each one sends — and where the link lives — decides how long the inbox needs to stay alive.

**Zoom.** Zoom webinars send a confirmation email with a unique join link, a reminder before the event, and usually a follow-up with a recording link afterwards. The join link is personal to your registration, so it should not be shared. Registration is by email, and a temporary inbox works for the whole chain as long as the expiry covers the recording. One caution: if the host requires authentication to join — a Zoom account, not just the link — a temporary inbox does not help with that half of the entry.

**Microsoft Teams.** Teams meetings and webinars send an invitation with a join link, and the link is typically also added to the calendar invite. Teams events are often tied to a work or school account, and some organisers restrict attendance to authenticated users from their organisation. For a public Teams webinar, a temp inbox covers the invitation and reminders; for an internal company event, use your work address, because the organiser will expect it.

**GoToWebinar.** GoToWebinar sends a confirmation with a join link, reminders before the session, and a follow-up with the recording and any handouts. The whole chain arrives by email, which makes it a good fit for a temporary inbox — provided the expiry runs past the recording, which can arrive a day or two after the event.

**Webex.** Cisco Webex events send a confirmation, reminders, and a post-event recording link, all by email. As with the others, the inbox must survive until the recording arrives, not just until the event starts.

**Google Meet.** Google Meet links are usually shared in a calendar invitation rather than a dedicated registration email, and many Meet events require a Google account to join. A temporary inbox can receive the invitation if one is sent, but it cannot substitute for the Google account when the host requires one. Check the entry requirements before registering with a disposable address.

**Eventbrite.** Eventbrite is different from the rest: it is a ticketing platform, and the ticket — with its order confirmation, event updates, and any refund or transfer — is tied to the email address. For a free online event, a temporary inbox works for the ticket and the join link. For a paid ticket, use a permanent address: the ticket is a purchase, and losing the email that holds it can mean losing entry.

The pattern across all six: the email chain is the easy part, and a temp inbox handles it. The exceptions are always the same two — an event that requires an account to join, and a ticket you paid for. Both of those need a permanent address.

## A workflow that keeps the link safe

Note the event details before you register: title, date and time including the time zone, organiser, and whether a recording will be sent. Time zones matter, because a session at 2:00 PM Eastern Time is 11:00 AM Pacific Time, and a reminder in the organiser's zone can be misread if you assume your own zone.

Create a temporary inbox for that event alone, with an expiry running past the event end, plus a margin for the recording. If the event is tomorrow, 24 hours may be enough. If it is in five days, 7 days may cover it. If it is in two weeks, or the recording arrives a week later, a single inbox may not cover the span; use a custom duration reaching the recording date, or a permanent address. Expired messages are assumed unrecoverable.

When confirmation arrives, save the joining link outside the inbox, in your own calendar or notes, so you can join even if you cannot open the inbox at the last minute. Save any access code the same way. Check the inbox after registration, the day before the event, and shortly before start time, because some organisers send a new link close to start time.

## Reminders, links, and recordings: a workflow per platform

The general workflow above works, but the recording step differs by platform, and the recording is the step most people forget to plan for.

| Platform | What arrives by email | Recording timing | Inbox must survive until |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zoom | Confirmation, reminders, recording link | Usually hours after, sometimes next day | The day after the event |
| Microsoft Teams | Invitation, reminders, recording link | Hours after, or when the organiser shares it | The day after the event |
| GoToWebinar | Confirmation, reminders, recording and handouts | Up to a day or two after | Two days after the event |
| Webex | Confirmation, reminders, recording link | Usually same day or next day | The day after the event |
| Google Meet | Calendar invitation, sometimes a recording link | Depends on the host sharing it | Until the host shares it, or use a permanent address |
| Eventbrite | Ticket, updates, join link | N/A — the ticket is the record | The event itself; paid tickets need a permanent address |

Two practical rules follow from this table. First, set the expiry to the recording date, not the event date: a 7-day inbox for an event in five days leaves two days of margin for the recording, while a 24-hour inbox for the same event leaves none. Second, save the recording link the moment it arrives — copy it into your notes while the inbox is still live, because a recording link that only exists inside an expired inbox is gone with it.

Some joining links are personal and should not be shared. Do not post a screenshot of your event inbox, or forward a personal link publicly. If a friend also wants to attend, ask them to register separately.

Recordings are the follow-up most people forget to plan for. If a recording is promised, note its expected delivery time. If that date falls after your inbox expiry, you will miss the link unless you saved another way to get it. For events where the recording is the main reason you registered, a permanent address is often safer. ## Managing several events at once

Give each event its own temporary inbox, and label it in your own notes with the event name, date, time zone, and organiser. Keep a short list, outside the inboxes, of events you have registered for, in date order. ## When an event deserves a permanent address

Use a permanent address when the event involves a paid ticket, a certificate, course credit, or proof of attendance, or when it is part of a course or membership you will return to. The same applies if attendees or organisers need to contact you after the event. Ask this test: if I lose access to this inbox the day after the event, will I lose anything I care about? If yes, use a permanent address.

## Checklist for your next event

- Have I noted the event date, time, and time zone, and added it to my own calendar?
- Does my expiry cover confirmation, reminders, the joining link, and any recording I want?
- Have I saved the joining link and any access code outside the temporary inbox?
- Is this event in its own inbox, separate from other events and signups?
- Do I know when to check the inbox: after registration, the day before, and shortly before start time?
- If I need a certificate, credit, or the recording after expiry, should I use a permanent address instead?

That checklist takes a minute, and it prevents the most common event mistake: registering with enthusiasm, then discovering on event day that the inbox, and the link with it, has already expired.
