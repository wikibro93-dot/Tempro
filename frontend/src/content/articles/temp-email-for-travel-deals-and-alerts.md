## A fare sale should not follow you home

You search for flights one evening, sign up for a price alert so you do not miss a drop, and then the trip is booked — or abandoned — while the emails keep coming. Months later you are still getting hotel offers for a city you never visited and "last chance" fares for dates that have already passed. The alert did its job in a single week, but the signup lived on far longer than the search.

A temporary inbox fits this pattern well, because a travel search usually has a clear end date. You need messages for a short window: confirmation of the alert, price-change notices, and perhaps a promo code. After that, you do not need the sender to keep writing to you. TempMail Pro gives you a free temporary address with no sign-in, so you can create an inbox for one trip search, watch it while you decide, and let it expire when the search is over.

## What a temporary inbox is good for here

Use it for the exploratory, short-lived parts of travel planning:

- Price-drop alerts for a specific route and date range you are actively comparing
- Deal newsletters you want to sample for one or two weeks before a trip
- Signups that unlock a fare list, a hotel coupon, or a destination guide
- Separate alerts for different trips, so a beach holiday and a work trip do not mix

This is different from a booking itself. If you are actually buying a ticket or reserving a room, the confirmation, itinerary changes, and check-in details should go to an address you will still control on travel day. Keep the temporary inbox for the search and the alerts, and keep the purchase on your permanent address.

## One inbox per trip

Mixing every destination into a single inbox recreates the clutter you were trying to avoid. A cleaner method is one inbox per trip, named for the trip itself. TempMail Pro supports [multiple inboxes](/blog/free-temp-mail-multiple-inboxes-guide) and custom names, so you can label one "Lisbon-May" and another "Chicago-Work" and tell at a glance which alert belongs where. The approach in the guide to [managing multiple temporary inboxes for different signups](/blog/how-to-manage-multiple-temp-emails) works well here: one purpose, one inbox, no guessing when a message arrives.

Name the inbox in plain words you will recognise later, without putting personal details in the name. When the trip search ends, delete the inbox or let it expire, and start fresh for the next search rather than reviving an old address for a new destination.

## Match the expiry to the search

TempMail Pro offers 10 minutes, 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days, or a custom duration. Pick the shortest duration that still covers the job:

| Search stage | Suggested expiry | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One fare check or a single download | 10 minutes to 1 hour | You only need a confirmation or a link |
| A weekend of comparing options | 24 hours | Alerts arrive across a day or two |
| A trip you are watching for a week | 7 days | Price movement takes time to show |
| An open-ended "someday" trip | Custom, with a review date | Revisit the search rather than leaving it open |

Expired inboxes and their messages are assumed unrecoverable, so save anything you still need before expiry. If a fare alert contains a code you plan to use, copy that code into your own notes while the inbox is still live. Do not leave the only copy of a booking reference inside an inbox that is about to expire.

## A practical workflow for a price alert

1. Decide the route, the dates, and the price you would actually pay, in dollars if that is how the fare is shown, before you sign up for anything.
2. Create a temporary inbox named for the trip and set an expiry that covers your decision window.
3. Sign up for the alert with that address, then confirm the signup from inside the temporary inbox.
4. Check the inbox once or twice a day while you are deciding, rather than leaving alerts unread until the fare has moved.
5. When the price hits your target, book with your permanent address, and save the confirmation outside the temporary inbox.
6. When the search ends, delete the temporary inbox. If the trip is postponed, create a fresh inbox when you restart the search.

Time zones matter more than people expect. A fare alert timed for morning Eastern time may land in the middle of the night in Pacific time, and a "today only" offer may expire sooner than it appears to. Act on the sender's stated deadline rather than assuming you have until the end of your own day. Once a trip moves from "maybe" to "booked," let your permanent inbox take over — confirmations and check-in updates need an address that will still exist on the day you travel.
