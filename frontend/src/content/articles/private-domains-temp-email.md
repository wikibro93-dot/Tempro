### Why Use a Private Temporary Email Domain?

Most people rely on free disposable email providers, but having your **own custom domain** gives you more **control, branding, and privacy**.

- Hide your real email
- Avoid spam and leaks
- Build trust for professional use

---

### Steps to Create Your Private Domain Email

1. **Buy a domain**  
   Register a domain from services like Namecheap, GoDaddy, or Google Domains.

2. **Set up email forwarding**  
   Use services like ImprovMX or ForwardEmail to forward mail to your real inbox.

3. **Add catch-all feature**  
   Configure a "catch-all" so any random email address under your domain works.  
   Example: anything@mydomain.com → forwards to your real inbox.

4. **Secure with filters**  
   Add filters or rules in Gmail/Outlook to keep spam away.

### Best Use Cases

- Signing up on websites without revealing your personal address
- Running multiple aliases for different platforms
- Professional privacy protection

✅ With a private domain, you are no longer dependent on free temp-mail services.

## A custom name is not a private domain — know which one you need

TempMail Pro lets you pick a custom name for a temporary inbox, which is useful for tracing signups. That is not the same as owning a private domain, and confusing the two leads to bad decisions. A custom name on a shared temp-mail domain still lives on someone else's domain, expires with the inbox, and cannot receive mail after expiry. A private domain is yours: you control its addresses, its forwarding, and how long they live.

| Need | Custom temp name | Your own private domain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Try a service for a day | Yes, ideal | Overkill |
| Trace which signup sent what | Yes, for the inbox lifetime | Yes, permanently |
| Receive mail months later | No — inbox expires | Yes, as long as you keep the domain |
| Look professional or branded | No | Yes |
| Effort and cost | None | Domain cost plus setup time |

## The catch-all trap

A catch-all address — where anything@yourdomain.com reaches you — is convenient and risky in equal measure. It means every guessable address works, including addresses spammers guess, addresses old signups leak, and addresses you never created. If you run a catch-all, pair it with filters from day one: route unknown senders to a separate folder, review it weekly, and retire addresses that start attracting spam instead of letting them accumulate. Without filters, a catch-all quietly becomes the same messy inbox a private domain was supposed to replace.

## When a private domain is overkill

For most people, most of the time, it is. If your actual need is trying trials, unlocking coupons, or testing signups, a temporary inbox with a custom name does the job with zero setup and zero ongoing cost. A private domain earns its keep only when you do this work repeatedly at volume — running many ongoing aliases, managing a business identity, or needing addresses that survive for months. Buying a domain for a single weekend trial is like renting an office to write one email.

## A sensible setup checklist

1. Buy a domain you can keep renewing — a lapsed domain can be re-registered by someone else, with your old addresses still pointing at it in old signups.
2. Set up forwarding to an inbox you actually check, not to an address you ignore.
3. Use a catch-all only with filters, or skip it and create addresses deliberately per signup.
4. Keep a list of which address went to which service, so a breach or spam wave can be traced and that one address retired.
5. Never use a private-domain address for banking or recovery-critical accounts unless that domain's email is itself secured and monitored long-term.
