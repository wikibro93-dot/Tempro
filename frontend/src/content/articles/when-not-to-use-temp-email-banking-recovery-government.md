## The question to ask before every signup

Temporary email is useful precisely because it does not last. That same quality makes it the wrong choice in a specific set of situations, and those situations are easy to recognise once you name them: any account where losing the address means losing the account, the money, the record, or the ability to prove who you are. If you cannot afford to lose access, do not sign up with an address designed to disappear.

This article is a checklist, not a warning against temp mail in general. Used for trials, alerts, and short-lived signups, a temporary inbox is a sensible tool. The skill is knowing where the line falls, and stopping before you cross it. TempMail Pro is free, needs no sign-in, and offers 10 minutes, 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days, or a custom expiry — but no expiry setting turns a temporary inbox into a permanent one, and expired inboxes and their messages are assumed unrecoverable.

## The decision checklist

Ask these questions before you enter a temporary address. If the answer to any of them is yes, use a permanent address you control.

- Will I need to reset the password for this account later?
- Does this account hold money, payment details, or a balance?
- Could I need to prove this account, transaction, or record months from now?
- Is this account tied to my identity with a bank, a government body, or a healthcare provider?
- Would losing this account lock me out of something else, such as a device or a work system?
- Is this the only recovery route for another important account?

A single "yes" is enough. You do not need three reasons to choose a permanent address; one is decisive, because recovery is the point at which a temporary inbox fails most quietly. The inbox expires, the reset link goes nowhere, and the account is effectively gone even though nothing was ever hacked or deleted by you.

## Banking and payments: always permanent

Bank accounts, payment apps, card accounts, and any service that moves or stores money should always use a permanent address. Transaction alerts, fraud warnings, statements, and dispute messages all travel by email, and a missed fraud alert because an inbox expired is a risk no convenience is worth. Payment services also use email to verify identity, confirm a new device, and recover an account. A temporary address breaks every one of those steps.

The same applies to [online shopping accounts](/blog/temp-email-for-online-shopping) where you have a saved payment method or an order you may need to return, refund, or claim on later. A one-off coupon signup is a different matter from a store account holding your card and your order history, and the line between them is whether money or a future claim is involved.

## Password recovery: the hidden dependency

Password recovery is the most common way people lose an account to a temporary inbox without realising it at signup. The signup itself works fine, the verification code arrives, and everything feels normal — until months later, when you forget the password and the reset link is sent to an address that no longer exists.

Treat any account as recovery-critical if it is the recovery address for something else, if it holds work you cannot recreate, or if it is the login for a device or a service you use daily. Your main email account itself is the clearest example: never register a primary email, a cloud account, or a phone account with a temporary address, because those accounts are the keys to everything else. The guide to whether [temp mail without login is safe or risky](/blog/temp-mail-without-login-safe-or-risky) covers this dependency in more detail, and it is worth reading before you use a temporary address for anything tied to a login you value.

## Government, healthcare, and official records

Government services, tax accounts, benefits, licences, and healthcare portals belong on a permanent address, without exception in this checklist. These services send appointment letters, renewal notices, eligibility decisions, and records requests, often with deadlines. Missing one because an inbox expired can mean a missed appointment, a lapsed renewal, or a delayed decision that takes weeks to untangle.

Healthcare adds a privacy dimension as well. Medical messages can contain sensitive personal information, and a temporary inbox is not a suitable place for them — not because the inbox is public, but because it is not an address you control over time, and health records need a stable, private channel you can return to. The same reasoning covers legal matters, insurance claims, and employment records: if the message may matter in a dispute or a claim later, keep it on a permanent address.

## A simple rule to remember

Before you paste a temporary address, pause for five seconds and ask: "If this inbox vanished tomorrow, what would I lose?" If the answer is nothing, or only a promo you did not want, go ahead. If the answer is access, money, a record, an appointment, or your identity with a service, stop and use your real address.

Temporary email is at its most useful when it is used deliberately, for the short jobs it was made for — and left alone for everything you cannot afford to lose.
