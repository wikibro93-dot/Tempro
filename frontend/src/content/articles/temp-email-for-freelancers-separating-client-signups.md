## When every client lives in the same inbox

A freelancer's inbox rarely belongs to one job. In a single week, you might register for a client's project board, try a design tool for a second client, join a file-sharing workspace for a third, and sign up for a trial that belongs to none of them yet. When all of those confirmations land in one personal inbox, a message for Client A is easy to miss, a message for Client B is easy to open in the wrong context, and a trial promo is easy to mistake for a client update.

Temporary inboxes solve a narrow but real part of this problem: separation. TempMail Pro is free, needs no sign-in, and supports multiple inboxes with custom names, so you can give each client or project its own signup address. The client work itself — contracts, invoices, and final deliverables — still belongs on your professional address. The exploratory signups around that work, the tool trials, the staging accounts, and the one-off platform registrations, are where a temporary inbox earns its place.

## Named platforms: what they actually ask for

The advice is different on each platform, because each one asks for email differently and each one treats a disposable address differently. The platforms below are the ones freelancers actually use; the pattern to learn is which of them will accept a temporary address and which ones should never get one.

**Upwork.** Your Upwork freelancer profile is your business identity: it holds your reputation, your job history, your payment method, and your client messages. Upwork requires email verification at signup, and it is the kind of account that must be recoverable for years. Never put your Upwork profile on a temporary address. A temp inbox can serve a peripheral signup around Upwork — a client asking you to join their external project board, or a tool trial for a specific Upwork job — but the profile itself belongs on your permanent professional address.

**Fiverr.** The same rule applies, for the same reasons: your Fiverr seller profile holds your gigs, your ratings, your orders, and your payouts. Fiverr requires email verification, and losing access to the account means losing the storefront. Use a permanent address for the profile, and reserve temp inboxes for the signups around a Fiverr order — a client's staging site, a file-sharing workspace, a feedback board.

**Toptal.** Toptal's screening process is longer and more involved than the others, with interviews and assessments spread over days or weeks. Any address used during screening needs to survive the whole process, and the eventual account holds client work and payments. A temporary inbox is the wrong tool for Toptal screening; use your professional address from the first application.

**Freelancer.com.** Freelancer.com requires email verification and holds your profile, bids, milestones, and payments. As with Upwork and Fiverr, the profile itself belongs on a permanent address. Temp inboxes fit the surrounding signups: a contest entry, a client's external tool, a one-off collaboration board.

**Contra and other portfolio platforms.** Contra and similar portfolio-first platforms are lighter than the marketplaces above, but the same principle holds: if the profile is how clients find you and how you get paid, it needs a permanent, recoverable address. A temp inbox fits a trial or a test account, not the public profile.

The honest summary: on every major freelance platform, the profile is the business and belongs on a permanent address. Temporary inboxes belong to the signups *around* the platform — the staging sites, feedback boards, file-sharing workspaces, and tool trials that belong to one client and one project.

## One inbox per client or project

Start with a simple rule: one inbox per client, or one per project if a single client has clearly separate jobs. Name each inbox for the work, not for the tool. "Acme-Website" tells you more at a glance than "design-tool-trial," especially when two clients use the same tool and their messages look alike. In TempMail Pro a custom name gets a random 4-character suffix appended, so the address is close to your chosen name rather than exactly it — the name still identifies the client in your inbox list.

A practical setup for a busy week might be: one inbox for Client A's website redesign, covering the staging site and feedback board; one for Client B's scheduling-tool trial; and one for your own business experiment, for a tool you are evaluating before recommending it to anyone. Personal signups stay entirely out of the client inboxes. This mirrors the system in the guide to [multiple free temp inboxes and how to organise them](/blog/free-temp-mail-multiple-inboxes-guide): the inbox label should answer "whose work is this?" before you even open the message.

## A per-client inbox and expiry system

The system is a small table you keep for yourself — one row per client, with the inbox name, the expiry, and the date the project ends. Here is what it looks like in practice:

| Client / project | Inbox name | Expiry | What lives there |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acme website redesign | `acme-website` | Custom, to the launch date | Staging site, feedback board, design-tool trial |
| Beta scheduling-tool trial | `beta-scheduler` | 7 days | Trial signup, reminder, cancellation confirmation |
| Client C file-sharing workspace | `client-c-files` | Custom, to the milestone date | Workspace invites, share notifications |
| Own tool evaluation | `eval-invoice-tool` | Custom 30 days | Trial, follow-up offers, comparison notes |
| Upwork / Fiverr / Toptal profile | — | Permanent address | Never a temporary inbox |

The expiry column is the point. Each inbox's expiry is matched to the project length: a one-week trial gets 7 days, a three-month project gets a custom duration to its end date, and a profile that must last years gets no temporary inbox at all. TempMail Pro's custom durations go up to 365 days, so a project running most of a year can be covered — but if the project runs longer than a year, the signup should have been on a permanent address from the start. When a project ends, delete its inbox rather than letting it linger; when a project extends, create a fresh inbox for the new phase rather than trying to extend the old one, since expired inboxes cannot be renewed.

## Steps for a new client signup

1. Create a temporary inbox named for the client or project, before you register anywhere for that job.
2. Set the expiry to cover the signup and testing phase. TempMail Pro offers 10 minutes, 1 hour, 24 hours, 7 days, or a custom duration up to 365 days.
3. Register with that address, confirm from inside the temporary inbox, and complete the setup you need.
4. Save any access link, invite detail, or setup note outside the temporary inbox — in your project notes — while the inbox is still live.
5. Tell the client which address you used for that platform, if they may need to re-invite you or check access later.
6. When the project phase ends, or when the signup becomes a permanent account, move it to a proper address and delete the temporary inbox.

Step 4 is the one freelancers skip most often, and it is the one that causes trouble later. Expired inboxes and their messages are assumed unrecoverable. If the only record of a staging login lives inside an expired inbox, you will be asking the client for a fresh invite and explaining why. Save the detail first, then let the inbox expire without worry.

## Choosing an expiry for freelance work

| Situation | Suggested expiry | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quick tool check for one client | 1 hour to 24 hours | Enough to confirm and test |
| Trial week for a tool you may recommend | 7 days | Matches a typical trial window |
| Staging or review phase for a project | Custom, tied to the project milestone | Review the date when the milestone moves |
| Ongoing client account or billing | Permanent address | Never a temporary inbox |

Time zones add a wrinkle for freelancers with clients across the US. A trial that expires at midnight Eastern time ends three hours earlier in Pacific time, and a client message sent late in one zone may look like it arrived the next day in another. Note the deadline in the sender's time zone, and set your own reminder a day earlier, so an expiry does not catch you mid-task.

## What stays on your professional address

Temporary inboxes are for signups, not for the business itself. Keep these on your permanent professional address, always: contracts and rate agreements; invoices, payment details, and tax documents; the main client communication thread; any account that holds client data, source files, or published work; password recovery for tools you rely on daily; and accounts you will still need after the project ends. That includes your Upwork, Fiverr, Toptal, and Freelancer.com profiles — all of them.

If a platform holds work you cannot recreate, or a message you may need to prove later, it does not belong on a disposable address. Some platforms also block disposable domains, which is useful information in itself: if a client's required tool rejects a temporary address, use a proper address for that tool and note the restriction for next time. A related caution applies to [job and application signups](/blog/free-temp-email-for-job-boards-guide), where the account may matter long after the first message arrives.

Used with discipline, temporary inboxes keep client worlds apart during the messy middle of a project — the trials and staging accounts around the real work — while the work itself, the money, and the relationships stay on addresses built to last.
